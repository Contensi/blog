Digital sovereignty has moved from a political buzzword to an operational necessity in 2026. According to a EuroCloud member survey, 45 percent of members name sovereignty as the year’s top trend topic - ahead of artificial intelligence.

## The "Deutschland-Stack" becomes binding

In March 2026, Germany’s IT Planning Council elevated the "Deutschland-Stack" from a strategic concept to the binding foundation for public-sector digitalisation: federal, state and local government agreed on a common platform core, a federal portfolio and binding technical standards. The Deutschland-Stack consistently builds on existing open standards - in particular the Sovereign Cloud Stack and the German Administration Cloud.

## Why open source plays the load-bearing role

Open, verifiable software standards enable interoperability and portability between cloud providers without locking into a single vendor. The Centre for Digital Sovereignty (ZenDiS), founded by the German federal government in 2022, drives exactly that: the availability of modern, capable open-source solutions for public administration.

## A framework for assessing cloud sovereignty

In April 2026, Germany’s Federal Office for Information Security (BSI) introduced the C3A Framework, which for the first time makes cloud sovereignty assessable - strategically, legally and technologically - across six dimensions. A signal that sovereignty is now treated as a measurable requirement, not just a statement of intent.

## What this means for the mid-market

These initiatives are aimed primarily at public administration - but the underlying idea transfers directly to mid-sized companies: those who bet on open, vendor-independent technologies - Proxmox instead of closed virtualisation platforms, Ceph instead of proprietary storage, Kubernetes/OpenShift instead of cloud-bound container services - don’t need to wait for a government funding programme to put digital sovereignty into practice. That combination of open standards and your own data centre is exactly the architecture we recommend at Contensi - not because it’s politically opportune, but because it hands companies back control over their own IT.
